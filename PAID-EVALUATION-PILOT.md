# Paid Evaluation / Replication Pilot

I am available for **bounded paid evaluation and replication work** on AI-agent reliability problems where a system can look successful while losing the actual objective, authority boundary, provenance chain, or recoverable state.

This is not an offer for open-ended free consulting. It is a small, inspectable engagement designed to produce a concrete result quickly.

## Good fit

Examples include:

- objective drift or proxy substitution under optimization pressure;
- agent handoffs where completed work, authority, or provenance can silently change;
- interrupted / partially observable tasks where the system can claim completion without preserving the principal's ability to inspect or correct it;
- evaluation designs that need hard gates, preserved negative results, or first-loss localization;
- independent reproduction of a bounded public result.

## Typical pilot

A pilot is scoped before work begins and may include:

1. a frozen test protocol and explicit claim boundary;
2. a small set of adversarial or matched-control runs;
3. preserved raw evidence / redacted traces where appropriate;
4. a failure taxonomy separating outcome validity, mechanism validity, and provenance / authority failures;
5. a short technical report with what the evidence supports, what it does not support, and the smallest next decision.

The goal is not to manufacture a positive result. A useful engagement may end in **PASS, FAIL, INDETERMINATE, or HOLD** if that is what the evidence warrants.

## Public evidence

This repository preserves a public example of the method: **Goodhart's Game 2.0**, including frozen protocol decisions and retained failure conditions rather than post-hoc repair toward a preferred outcome.

## Provenance and IP boundary

Public artifacts remain governed by their existing repository / publication terms. A pilot does **not** automatically grant access to unpublished architecture, private source material, internal continuity records, or unrelated methods.

Before non-public work begins, scope, deliverables, attribution, confidentiality, and any transfer or license of new work should be agreed in writing. Unless a separate agreement says otherwise, background methods and pre-existing work remain background material rather than becoming part of the client's ownership simply because they were used during an evaluation.

This page is a public scope statement and provenance marker, **not a substitute for a contract or legal advice**.

## Starting a conversation

If you have a real evaluation problem that fits the scope above, send a **specific system / workflow / failure claim** and the decision you need the evaluation to support. I can then propose the smallest paid pilot that would produce useful evidence.
